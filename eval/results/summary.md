### Reading the table

- `none` never intervenes, so it scores every should-not-fire case correctly and every should-fire case wrong. It is the floor.
- `exact_dict` handles spellings it was explicitly taught and fails on spellings it was not. Its false interventions are the expensive kind: it rewrites the fruit.
- `phonetic` generalises to unseen spellings *and* declines to act when the evidence does not support it. Both halves are the product.

## Failures (1)

### `codemix-devanagari-should-fire` *(known_hard, expected)*

- outcome: `missed_intervention`
- input: `कीवी सर्विस ठीक है।`
- expected: `Kivi सर्विस ठीक है।`
- actual: `कीवी सर्विस ठीक है।`
- why: Identity now crosses scripts, but context does not, and this case pins the difference. 'कीवी' is a homophone of the fruit, so the guard demands supporting context. The supporting word here is 'सर्विस' -- a transliterated English loanword that skeletonises to 'sarvis', while the context term learned from English text skeletonises to 'servike'. They do not meet. The result is conservative rather than wrong: in Devanagari, homophone terms stay guarded, while unambiguous names like आदित्य resolve normally. Fixing it properly means learning context terms per script, or transliteration-aware loanword matching. Reported as a failure.
- system said: 'कीवी' is an ordinary English word and nothing in this sentence belongs to 'Kivi'; leaving it alone

## Reproducibility

Per-case artifacts under `eval/results/cases/` contain no timing data and are byte-identical across runs on the same commit. After re-running this harness, `git diff -- eval/results/cases` should report **no changes at all**; only the measured latency numbers in `results.json` and in this file will move. Timing is reported once, from the dedicated warm-path measurement above, rather than as 56 noisy single-shot samples.
